# Grownetics Academy curriculum module map

**Status (2026-09-28):** proposed, unpiloted planning blueprint. These 27 curriculum IDs are stable identifiers *within this proposal*, not academy catalog numbers, web routes, published lessons, approved kits, or a selected classroom pilot. The user has approved ongoing documentation and the [licensing boundary](../LICENSE.md), not every design below. See the [living outline](curriculum-outline.md) and [decision log](curriculum-decisions.md) for the distinction between direction, proposals, and open decisions.

## How to use the map

Discover → Measure → Control → Integrate → Investigate describes increasing system scope, **not five required courses**. A module may be adopted alone if learners demonstrate its listed prerequisites, including equivalent prior competence. A biology investigation need not pass through coding or a tent. A recorded-data alternative can establish interpretation outcomes, but not hands-on sensor checkout, wiring, calibration, or equipment commissioning. Each entry is a planning brief rather than a complete student lesson or teacher guide.

**Notation:** `Contact` is an estimated amount of facilitated instructional time; `elapsed` is additional estimated calendar time for growing or observation, not seat time. `0` means no growing interval is needed. Estimates assume accessible materials and ordinary classroom pacing; local schedules and plant development will change them. Minimum materials name *capabilities*, not selected products, purchase specifications, or prices. Printed records, instructor-provided datasets, and simulations should identify their provenance and what they cannot demonstrate. Classroom work needs local supervision, accessible alternatives, equipment approval, and risk review before use. No activity authorizes mains wiring, unattended unsafe actuators, automatic chemical dosing, CO₂ enrichment, or consumption of experimental plants. Where an activity mentions a real instrument, teachers must confirm its range, maintenance and safe handling; students must label proxy measurements as proxies.

**Readiness terms:** *Proposed* (all 27 entries here) means scope and evidence are planned, not fully authored or approved. *Authored draft* would require usable student materials, teacher preparation, accommodations, assessment criteria, alternative delivery, sources, and site-specific safety instructions. *Classroom-piloted* would require documented use and revisions, not merely a demo. *Publication-ready* would additionally require editorial, accessibility, licensing, safety, and local applicability review. No proposed entry below claims any of those later states or exact standards alignment. The optional pathways are planning routes, not tested schedules.

## Discover — living systems and evidence

**FND-01 — Plants as Living Systems** · Prerequisites: none. **Question:** What changes show that a plant is living and responding to its environment? **Outcomes:** identify structures and needs; distinguish a direct observation from a claim about a cause. **Practice:** sketch and date seed-to-seedling changes and describe likely roles of roots, stems, and leaves. **Minimum/alternative:** seeds, safe growing medium and light; or sequenced, dated plant images for observational interpretation only. **Evidence:** annotated journal with a claim tied to two dated observations. **Time (estimate):** contact 45–60 min; elapsed 1–2 weeks of brief observations. **Limits:** wash hands after handling media; growth alone does not reveal which need limited growth.

**FND-02 — Asking Testable Questions** · Prerequisites: FND-01. **Question:** What comparison could distinguish a proposed explanation from another? **Outcomes:** state a measurable question, comparison, outcome, and factors held reasonably consistent; identify when a question is not experimentally testable as posed. **Practice:** plan a low-risk seed-growth comparison without claiming that a single pot establishes an effect. **Minimum/alternative:** paper planning sheet or supplied scenarios; living plants optional. **Evidence:** a comparison plan with a predicted observation and at least one confounder. **Time (estimate):** contact 45–60 min; elapsed 0. **Limits:** plan first; no deprivation likely to cause unsafe conditions, and a proposed experiment is not evidence of causality.

**FND-03 — Measurements, Units & Uncertainty** · Prerequisites: none. **Question:** How much confidence should we place in a number? **Outcomes:** record units, resolution, time, and repeat readings; separate variation from an apparent trend. **Practice:** measure container water volume or seedling height repeatedly and compare techniques. **Minimum/alternative:** ruler or marked measuring vessel; supplied repeated-measurement records for analysis only. **Evidence:** dated table with units and a justified uncertainty statement. **Time (estimate):** contact 45–60 min; elapsed 0 or short repeat visits. **Limits:** avoid sharp glass and spills; more decimal places do not make an instrument more accurate.

**FND-04 — From Records to Evidence** · Prerequisites: FND-02 and FND-03. **Question:** What do our observations support, and what remains unknown? **Outcomes:** represent a time series with units in a graph or accessible ordered table, describe missing values, and distinguish correlation, monitoring, and controlled comparison. **Practice:** interpret a small growing log, including an absent observation or changed procedure. **Minimum/alternative:** paper graph or accessible table and dated manual or supplied records; no code required. **Evidence:** graph/table and claim–evidence–limitations explanation. **Time (estimate):** contact 60–90 min; elapsed 0. **Limits:** do not infer treatment effects from one monitored plant or interpolate gaps as if measured.

**BIO-01 — Plant Nutrition and Matter Flows** · Prerequisites: FND-01 and FND-03. **Question:** Where do plant mass and required nutrients come from? **Outcomes:** distinguish light energy, carbon from air, water, and mineral nutrients; recognize that visual symptoms have multiple possible causes. **Practice:** trace matter and energy through a plant-system diagram and inspect non-diagnostic symptom photographs. **Minimum/alternative:** paper diagrams and sourced images; plants optional. **Evidence:** annotated flow model and two alternative explanations for one symptom. **Time (estimate):** contact 60–90 min; elapsed 0. **Limits:** no tasting plants or diagnosing a specific deficiency from appearance alone.

**BIO-02 — Roots, Water and Growing Media** · Prerequisites: FND-01 and FND-03. **Question:** How do roots obtain water and air in different media? **Outcomes:** explain water retention, drainage, and aeration trade-offs; report medium-dependent measurements. **Practice:** compare drainage and retained water in two school-approved media. **Minimum/alternative:** small containers, water and approved media; or supplied measurements for reasoning, not handling competence. **Evidence:** mass/volume record and explanation of a trade-off. **Time (estimate):** contact 60–90 min; elapsed 0–2 days for repeat checks. **Limits:** control dust/spills and follow local media hygiene; one moisture-sensor calibration does not transfer between media.

## Measure — instrumentation and trustworthy records

**MSR-01 — Safe Circuits & Microcontrollers** · Prerequisites: FND-03. **Question:** How can a low-voltage circuit convey a measurable signal safely? **Outcomes:** identify power, ground, input, and output roles; check a protected circuit against a diagram before energizing. **Practice:** supervised low-voltage circuit and microcontroller input check. **Minimum/alternative:** approved protected low-voltage trainer and instructor; paper circuit-tracing substitutes only for conceptual outcomes. **Evidence:** checked diagram and observed input response with a recorded safe shutdown. **Time (estimate):** contact 90–120 min; elapsed 0. **Limits:** no mains, exposed high-current supplies, or water near energized breadboards; a simulation does not prove physical checkout.

**MSR-02 — Sensors & Data Logging** · Prerequisites: MSR-01 and FND-03. **Question:** What does a sensor actually measure, and when? **Outcomes:** name a sensor's measured quantity, unit, time basis, and at least two likely error sources; save a dated series. **Practice:** after hardware checkout, log one safe environmental quantity and compare spot readings with a reference method. **Minimum/alternative:** approved low-voltage sensor/logging capability plus reference; supplied logs permit interpretation but not sensor competence. **Evidence:** timestamped readings and brief discrepancy analysis. **Time (estimate):** contact 90–120 min; elapsed 1–3 days of brief logging. **Limits:** avoid wet electrical connections; uncalibrated relative light readings are not photosynthetic photon flux measurements.

**MSR-03 — Calibration, Drift and Missing Data** · Prerequisites: MSR-02. **Question:** When should a measurement be trusted or rejected? **Outcomes:** compare against a suitable reference, document offset or drift, and flag out-of-range or missing readings without silently replacing them. **Practice:** review a sensor against a reference at two conditions or audit a supplied calibration log. **Minimum/alternative:** checked low-voltage instrument and appropriate reference, or labeled recorded calibration series for analysis only. **Evidence:** dated calibration record, quality flags, and decision about use. **Time (estimate):** contact 90–120 min; elapsed 0–1 week for drift checks. **Limits:** two points do not establish accuracy over every condition; do not adjust safety-critical equipment from unverified readings.

**MSR-04 — Data Stewardship and Interpretation** · Prerequisites: FND-04 and MSR-02. **Question:** Could another group understand and reanalyze this record? **Outcomes:** preserve units, timestamps/time zone, provenance, missingness and revisions; explain a plausible alternative interpretation. **Practice:** reconcile two logs and produce a small, documented table and chart. **Minimum/alternative:** paper log sheets or spreadsheet with supplied records; no live device needed for data-handling outcomes. **Evidence:** annotated dataset plus claim and counterexample. **Time (estimate):** contact 90–120 min; elapsed 0. **Limits:** remove personal information; never silently fill gaps or present copied weather data without source terms.

**SW-01 — Programming a Reproducible Logger** · Prerequisites: MSR-02. **Question:** How can another learner reproduce and safely change the processing of the same readings? **Outcomes:** implement or adapt a small teaching-only logger/parser with explicit units, timestamps and missing-value handling; use variables, conditionals and iteration; preserve a versioned source and input record; check expected behavior before and after a change. **Practice:** transform a documented sample stream into a reviewable table, compare it with raw readings, and retain a change history plus checks for valid, missing and malformed records. **Minimum/alternative:** offline computer and provided synthetic records; annotated pseudocode and paper traces cover algorithmic reasoning, not code execution or version-control competence. **Evidence:** runnable teaching example, reproducible input/output instructions, change record and behavior checks, or explicitly labeled conceptual alternatives. **Time (estimate):** contact 180–240 min; elapsed 0. **Limits:** never deploy classroom code as production control firmware; do not publish device credentials or learner data. Original teaching examples follow the MIT scope; product code is excluded.

## Control — interventions with safe failure

**CTL-01 — Feedback, Thresholds and Hysteresis** · Prerequisites: MSR-02 and FND-04. **Question:** Why might a controller oscillate around a target? **Outcomes:** distinguish open loop from feedback and justify a deadband using noisy measurements. **Practice:** run a paper or software simulation of a low-risk fan/temperature loop, then compare traces. **Minimum/alternative:** graph paper and supplied trace suffice; no actuator required. **Evidence:** annotated state/trace with a response to an invalid reading. **Time (estimate):** contact 90–120 min; elapsed 0. **Limits:** a simulated loop does not certify climate performance; invalid readings should prompt a defined safe state, not blind switching.

**CTL-02 — Actuators, Mechanisms and Interlocks** · Prerequisites: MSR-01. **Question:** How does a commanded output become a physical change, and how can it stop? **Outcomes:** distinguish command, actuator, process, and feedback; demonstrate a supervised stop/interlock on a protected low-voltage mechanism. **Practice:** map and exercise a low-voltage motor or pump trainer without connecting it to crops. **Minimum/alternative:** approved protected trainer; paper fault-path analysis supports conceptual outcomes only. **Evidence:** system diagram and observed stop test or labeled simulated response. **Time (estimate):** contact 90–120 min; elapsed 0. **Limits:** no mains switching on breadboards; protect moving parts and water/electrical separation; a paper interlock is not verified hardware.

**CTL-03 — Nutrient Solutions and Root-Zone Chemistry** · Prerequisites: BIO-01 and FND-03. **Question:** What can pH and electrical conductivity tell us about a root zone? **Outcomes:** explain pH as a condition affecting availability and EC as bulk ionic conductivity; identify measurements unable to specify individual nutrients. **Practice:** interpret labeled sample pH/EC records, or supervised comparison of school-approved, nonhazardous solutions. **Minimum/alternative:** provided records suffice; hands-on route requires suitable maintained instruments and approved solutions. **Evidence:** interpretation with units, calibration status, and an unresolved alternative. **Time (estimate):** contact 90–120 min; elapsed 0. **Limits:** no chemical dosing, ingestion or concentrated reagents; EC is not an individual-nutrient analyzer and pH is not a nutrient inventory.

**CTL-04 — Light, Photoperiod and Plant Response** · Prerequisites: BIO-01 and FND-03. **Question:** How do duration and measured light quantity differ? **Outcomes:** distinguish photoperiod from intensity and explain limits of relative light readings for plant-light decisions. **Practice:** compare documented light schedules and chart exposure duration without claiming crop performance. **Minimum/alternative:** paper schedule and sourced light records; supervised approved lighting optional. **Evidence:** schedule comparison and measurement-limit note. **Time (estimate):** contact 60–90 min; elapsed 0–1 week if observing plants. **Limits:** do not equate a phone lux reading with calibrated photosynthetic photon measurements; avoid glare, hot fixtures, and unsafe mains handling.

**CTL-05 — Safe Single-Process Automation** · Prerequisites: CTL-01 and CTL-02. **Question:** Can one controlled process stop safely when its input fails? **Outcomes:** define set range, permitted actuator states, sensor-fault response, and manual override; compare manual and automatic traces. **Practice:** simulate a fault, then optionally supervise one approved protected low-voltage process. **Minimum/alternative:** paper/software trace permits design assessment; physical operation requires an approved enclosed trainer. **Evidence:** operating envelope, fault trace, and actual stop observation only when hardware used. **Time (estimate):** contact 120–180 min; elapsed 0–3 days of supervised observation if used. **Limits:** simulated fault injection, not hazardous real failure; no unattended irrigation, chemical dosing or claim of complete environmental control.

## Integrate — coupled environment and maintainable systems

**INT-01 — Climate, Moist Air and Loads** · Prerequisites: FND-03 and BIO-01. **Question:** Why does changing ventilation also change moisture and energy needs? **Outcomes:** describe temperature/humidity coupling and distinguish crop-zone measurements from room or weather observations. **Practice:** reason from a documented day/night climate record and simplified heat/moisture flow sketch. **Minimum/alternative:** paper records and plots, with optional approved instruments. **Evidence:** annotated balance and two constraints on a proposed setting. **Time (estimate):** contact 120–180 min; elapsed 0. **Limits:** simplified estimates are not HVAC design approval; reported humidity alone does not quantify all moisture or condensation risks.

**INT-02 — Water, Energy and Resource Trade-offs** · Prerequisites: INT-01 and BIO-02. **Question:** Which resource is saved, and what might that saving cost elsewhere? **Outcomes:** bound a simple water and energy account with units; compare two designs under stated assumptions. **Practice:** account for irrigation volume, runoff/reuse and estimated equipment energy on a scenario sheet. **Minimum/alternative:** supplied scenario data and calculator; a live system is not required. **Evidence:** balances and a trade-off explanation showing uncertainty. **Time (estimate):** contact 90–120 min; elapsed 0. **Limits:** do not claim measured savings from modeled values; reuse water safely under local procedures, not as a food-safety guarantee.

**INT-03 — Integrating Instruments and Controls** · Prerequisites: CTL-05 and MSR-04. **Question:** Can measurements, commands and logs be traced through a whole system? **Outcomes:** map interfaces and authority boundaries; reconcile a sensor reading, command, observed response and log event. **Practice:** simulate integration with a documented interface, or use a separately approved teaching interface if verified. **Minimum/alternative:** printed interface contract and event logs suffice for reasoning; real integration needs compatible approved equipment. **Evidence:** sequence diagram and discrepancy report. **Time (estimate):** contact 120–180 min; elapsed 0. **Limits:** no presumed Grownetics API, compatibility or software rights; isolate teaching systems from production controls and protect credentials.

**INT-04 — Reliability, Maintenance and Recovery** · Prerequisites: CTL-05 and MSR-03. **Question:** How will an operator notice a failure and restore safe operation? **Outcomes:** prioritize plausible sensor, actuator, water and power faults; define checks, safe states and recovery evidence. **Practice:** tabletop failure review and simulated alarm/stop/restart sequence. **Minimum/alternative:** documented subsystem map and fault cards; approved trainer optional. **Evidence:** maintenance checklist and recovery record distinguishing detected from undetected faults. **Time (estimate):** contact 120–180 min; elapsed 0. **Limits:** faults are simulated or instructor-approved; no intentional hazardous failure, and a checklist does not establish equipment certification.

**INT-05 — Commissioning an Engineered Growing Environment** · Prerequisites: INT-01, INT-03 and INT-04. **Question:** Does the integrated system operate within its documented limits? **Outcomes:** specify an operating envelope, compare manual baseline with staged subsystem operation, and document unresolved faults. **Practice:** carry out a tabletop commissioning review; a tent exercise only after site and interface approval. **Minimum/alternative:** representative system records and diagrams for design reasoning; actual commissioning requires supervised, approved tent equipment. **Evidence:** signed-off *exercise* checklist, baseline/control comparison and open-issue log; physical competence only from an actual checked system. **Time (estimate):** contact 180–240 min; elapsed 1–2 weeks of optional growing observations. **Limits:** not a certified commercial cultivation design or proof of independent control of every variable; approved enclosed mains interfaces and independent protections are required for real equipment.

## Investigate — reproducible research and advanced design

**INV-01 — Experimental Design and Variation** · Prerequisites: FND-04 and BIO-02; additional entry skills are averages and graphical comparison of variation. **Question:** Which differences can a treatment plausibly explain? **Outcomes:** define the experimental unit, comparison, replication, random assignment and positional blocking; summarize an observed difference alongside variation rather than reporting a winner alone. **Practice:** design and critique a medium/watering comparison, then summarize provided replicate records or records from an approved study. **Minimum/alternative:** paper scenarios, calculator and documented data; live growing optional. **Evidence:** protocol sketch, treatment-allocation plan, unit-of-analysis justification and effect/variation summary with units and limits. **Time (estimate):** contact 180–240 min; elapsed 2–4 weeks if an approved grow is conducted. **Limits:** the treatment unit may be a pot, tray or shared reservoir, depending on assignment; repeated sensor readings and multiple plants sharing one treated unit are not independent treatment replicates. Small samples and uncontrolled gradients limit causal claims; experimental plants are not for consumption.

**INV-02 — Dynamics and Advanced Control** · Prerequisites: CTL-01 and MSR-03; additional entry skills are signed error, ratios and reading time-series graphs. **Question:** When do delay, accumulated error or actuator limits make a simple controller inadequate? **Outcomes:** estimate response delay from a trace; explain proportional, integral and derivative (PID) actions using discrete differences and sums; compare hysteresis, PID and a constrained look-ahead strategy under uncertainty. **Practice:** examine a simulated delayed climate process, actuator saturation and integral windup; compare supplied response traces and choose a short sequence of permitted actions against a stated forecast. **Minimum/alternative:** paper traces and calculator or an offline simulation; no tent or calculus course required for the conceptual comparison. **Evidence:** response comparison, justified controller choice, explicit constraints, and a fault response; distinguish classroom look-ahead reasoning from an implemented model-predictive controller. **Time (estimate):** contact 240–360 min; elapsed 0. **Limits:** toy dynamics do not authorize tuning real ventilation or heating; model parameters and safe settings may not transfer to another enclosure.

**INV-03 — Software, Networks and Data Integrity** · Prerequisites: SW-01 and MSR-04. **Question:** Who may issue a command, and can we trust the record afterward? **Outcomes:** diagram a teaching-device data flow, identify authentication/authorization and integrity boundaries, and recover a documented sample after an interruption. **Practice:** use synthetic offline messages to model accepted, rejected and missing events. **Minimum/alternative:** offline computer or paper event cards; no internet or product credentials needed. **Evidence:** threat/permission map and reconciled event history. **Time (estimate):** contact 120–180 min; elapsed 0. **Limits:** never expose student identifiers, real secrets or production endpoints; a teaching security exercise is not a penetration test or production certification.

**INV-04 — Climate and Resource Optimization** · Prerequisites: INT-02 and FND-04; additional entry skills are unit conversion, ratios and simple algebra. **Question:** What is the best feasible compromise under stated constraints? **Outcomes:** distinguish an objective from a hard constraint; compare water, energy and crop-environment objectives; identify trade-offs where no option is best on every measure; test sensitivity to assumptions. **Practice:** define feasible operating choices for two sourced climate scenarios, compare resource use and cost under explicit input assumptions, and explain when a changed assumption alters the preferred choice. **Minimum/alternative:** sourced scenarios, calculator and paper or an offline spreadsheet; no tent needed. **Evidence:** objective/constraint statement, transparent trade-off table, rejected infeasible options and a sensitivity explanation. **Time (estimate):** contact 180–240 min; elapsed 0. **Limits:** an optimum depends on the model, constraints and chosen priorities; it is not measured plant yield, site engineering approval or an economic promise.

**INV-05 — Space-Agriculture Substrate Investigation** · Prerequisites: INV-01, BIO-01 and BIO-02. **Question:** Could a specified simulant treatment improve establishment relative to an appropriate control? **Outcomes:** distinguish simulant from lunar regolith; design replicated comparisons and separate plant growth, substrate conditioning and remediation claims. **Practice:** first develop an analysis plan from documented reference and example records; actual simulant trials require the exact material and an approved partner/school protocol. **Minimum/alternative:** published research summaries and labeled sample data; no simulant is required for design/interpretation outcomes. **Evidence:** comparison plan naming experimental units, endpoints, uncertainty and unsupported inferences. **Time (estimate):** contact 180–240 min; elapsed 3–6 weeks only if a separately approved growth trial occurs. **Limits:** no hands-on simulant without its safety data, dust controls and disposal approval; plants are not food, sensor behavior must be checked in that medium, and neither NASA endorsement nor lunar-habitat performance follows from a classroom result.

**INV-06 — Reproducible Claims and Communication** · Prerequisites: INV-01. **Question:** Can another team tell what we did and what the evidence cannot show? **Outcomes:** distinguish protocol from result, disclose missing data and departures, and communicate an appropriately bounded conclusion. **Practice:** assemble an evidence packet from one's approved study or supplied anonymous study records. **Minimum/alternative:** paper records and source notes; no growing system needed. **Evidence:** methods, data dictionary, figure and limitations statement enabling independent critique. **Time (estimate):** contact 90–120 min; elapsed 0. **Limits:** protect learner privacy and third-party data rights; do not relabel a proposed or simulated investigation as measured findings.

## Existing academy resources and provenance

The two **existing** online modules are separate from the 27 proposed curriculum entries and retain their current publication states and URLs. [The Evolution of CEA Control](../modules/cea-control/module.js) is **published** and can deepen CTL-01, CTL-05 or INV-02 discussions of coupled effects, control and uncertainty; it is professional-facing, not a prerequisite for P-01. [Climate, Psychrometrics & CEA Design](../modules/climate-design/module.js) is a **publicly listed draft accessed through preview mode**, relevant to INT-01, INT-02 and INV-04; its appearance here does not publish it, validate a new lesson, or replace site-specific design review. Catalog numbering is not these curriculum IDs.

Grounding and further reading already identified in the [outline](curriculum-outline.md): the [NGSS three-dimensional framework](https://www.nextgenscience.org/three-dimensional-learning) informs the emphasis on practices, ideas and crosscutting reasoning but **no specific performance-expectation alignment is claimed**. The [NASA account of growth in Apollo regolith](https://www.nasa.gov/humans-in-space/scientists-grow-plants-in-lunar-soil/) and its [reported study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-022-03334-8) motivate cautious space-agriculture questions; they do not validate an unspecified simulant treatment or imply sponsorship. Third-party publications, images, software and datasets keep their own rights and attribution.
